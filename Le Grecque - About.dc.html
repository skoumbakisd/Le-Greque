Twelve summers
of knowing who to call

Le Grecque is one person, working with a small circle of captains, chefs, drivers, and villa owners across western Crete. Not an agency, and deliberately not a large one.

Why one person, and not a company

Concierge work fails at the handover. A request passes from an inbox to a coordinator to a supplier, and by the time it reaches the person with the boat, the reason behind it has been lost. Here there is no handover. The person you message is the person who calls the harbour.

That sets a limit on how many guests can be looked after at once, particularly in July and August. It is the reason returning clients tend to write months ahead, and the reason the answer is occasionally no.

What the relationships are worth

A restaurant in the old port turns away dozens of walk-ins on an August evening. It holds a handful of tables back for people it trusts to arrive on time, order properly, and treat the staff well. Access is not bought. It is accumulated, one season at a time, and it can be lost.

The same is true of the captain who will move a charter by an hour, and the family who will open the winery on a day it is closed. Those favours exist because she has spent twelve years not spending them carelessly.
